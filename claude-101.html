What is Claude

Meet Claude

What is Claude

Claude is more than a chatbot — it's an AI assistant designed to be your thinking partner.

By the end of this lesson you'll be able to:

  • Explain what Claude is and the principles that guide its design
  • Describe Claude's core capabilities and how it differs from a simple chatbot
  • Identify the different ways to access Claude (web, desktop, and mobile)

  • Built to be helpful, harmless, and honest

    Claude is guided by principles that help it avoid toxic or discriminatory outputs, avoid helping humans engage in illegal or unethical activities, and broadly behave as a helpful, honest, and harmless AI system. This approach, called Constitutional AI, means Claude is trained to align with human values and operate transparently.

  • More than a chatbot

    Claude handles a wide variety of conversational and text-processing tasks with a high degree of reliability and predictability — summarization, search, creative and collaborative writing, Q&A, coding, and more. Think of Claude as a thinking partner who can help you tackle complex problems and work through challenging situations, not just answer simple questions.

  • Steerable and collaborative

    Claude can take direction on personality, tone, and behavior. Customers report Claude is much less likely to produce harmful outputs, easier to converse with, and more steerable — so you get your desired output with less effort.

  • Available wherever you work

    Claude apps are available on Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans. Your conversations, projects, memory, and preferences sync across all devices when you're signed in — desk or on the go, web, desktop, or mobile.

Try it · sort six requests

Which of these needs a thinking partner? The line was never the topic — reviews, invoices, exchange rates could all be typed into a search box. What separates the two columns is whether the answer needs you in it: your draft, your last deck, your read on the customer. A search box takes a question. Claude takes the situation.

A search box handles these

  • "What's the exchange rate from US dollars to euros today?"
  • "What's the difference between a purchase order and an invoice?"
  • "How many working days are there between March 3 and March 24?"

These need Claude — and you

  • "Rewrite my update below so the delay reads as a decision, not an apology."
  • "Best structure for a quarterly business review presentation."
  • "I've pasted a customer's complaint thread below. Help me work out what they're actually asking for before I reply."

You'd have sorted 3 of 6 this way going in. The ones that trip most people are the review structure and the invoice question — same need, typed as a query or handed over as a situation. "Best structure for a QBR presentation" reads like a search, but the good answer depends on your audience, your data, and your last deck — that's a Claude-and-you question wearing a search-box disguise. The invoice-versus-purchase-order question, on the other hand, has one factual answer that doesn't need your context at all.

Where Claude excels

  • Writing and content creation — social posts, professional emails, complex reports. Because Claude takes direction on personality and tone, you iterate together on structure and clarity until your voice comes through.
  • Research and analysis — explore angles, compile findings, analyze data. Claude's large context window ingests 200K+ tokens (about 500 pages or more), with up to 1M tokens on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans on supported models.
  • Coding assistance — one of Claude's greatest strengths: writing, debugging, and explaining code across languages.
  • Problem-solving and reasoning — complex cognitive tasks, math, strategy. Claude can respond near-instantly or take time to reason first — a capability called Thinking.
  • Learning new things — Learning mode guides your reasoning process rather than handing over answers, helping build critical thinking skills.

Ways to access Claude

Claude is the intelligence — the same intelligence is available across several interfaces, each suited to different tasks. This course focuses on claude.ai, the primary way most people work with Claude, but the same Claude also shows up in Claude Code (agentic coding), @Claude in Slack, Claude Design, and Claude for Microsoft 365 (Excel, PowerPoint, Word, Outlook).

Your first conversation with Claude

First conversation

Your first conversation with Claude

Claude is a powerful, intelligent collaborator that amplifies your capabilities. Claude brings AI intelligence — you bring the context and expertise that makes the work meaningful.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Start a new conversation with Claude and navigate the interface
  • Write effective prompts using clear, specific language
  • Upload files and images to provide Claude with additional context
  • Use follow-up messages to iterate and refine Claude's responses

The best approach when speaking to Claude is like you would a coworker — naturally, concisely, and conversationally. Prompts range from simple questions (brainstorming feature names) to complex requests to co-create files.

Three elements of an effective prompt

1 · Setting the stage
What is your role and what are your objectives? Is there context about your work Claude should know?
2 · Defining the task
What action do you want Claude to take — write, analyze, build, something else?
3 · Specifying rules
What style or tone do you want? Are there examples you can attach to show Claude what you're looking for?
"I'm the marketing lead at an indie streaming startup, and we're preparing an investor pitch deck for Series A investors. Can you research the current state of the independent film streaming market and identify key trends, competitor positioning, and growth opportunities? Use current web research with citations and structure it as a professional report of up to 5 pages, with an executive summary, market analysis, competitive landscape, and growth opportunities."

Setting the stage: an investor pitch deck for a new indie streaming app. Defining the task: research the market with specific angles (trends, competitors, opportunities). Specifying rules: current web research with citations, structured as a professional report.

Go deeper. This framework is adapted from the 4D Framework for AI Fluency — Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence — developed by Professor Rick Dakan (Ringling College of Art and Design) and Professor Joseph Feller (University College Cork). Take the free AI Fluency course to go deeper.

Adding context

Uploads, connectors, and custom preferences give Claude more context. Claude can analyze text and visual elements — images, charts, graphics — in PDFs and other documents (PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, PNG, JPEG). Practical uses: summarize an uploaded document, describe an image, find trends in a spreadsheet, explain or debug uploaded code.

Pro tip. Settings → General → "What personal preferences should Claude consider?" sets preferences that apply to every conversation.

Iterating on Claude's responses

  • Ask follow-up questions — "Can you expand on the second point?" or "That's helpful, but can you make it more concise?"
  • Provide feedback — "This is good, but the tone is too formal. Can you make it more conversational?"
  • Redirect or restart — "Actually, I was asking about X, not Y." Worst case, start a new chat to fully refresh the context.

Pro tip. Click the pencil icon on any of your messages to edit and resubmit — useful when refining a request rather than adding a new message.

Personalizing Claude

Memory
Automatically saves key context from your conversations — your role, preferences, past decisions, working style — so you don't repeat yourself every new chat. Review, edit, or delete anything Claude remembers in Settings; it syncs across devices.
Styles
Customize how Claude communicates. Choose a preset (concise, formal, explanatory) or describe your own custom style. Once set, it applies across all conversations automatically.

Put it into practice. Before moving on, try prompting Claude with a question or task. If you need an idea to get started, explore our use-case gallery.

Getting better results

You'll likely hit moments where the response isn't quite what you expected. That's normal — and an opportunity to refine your approach.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

The iteration mindset

Your first prompt rarely produces a perfect result — that's okay. Treat it as the start of a conversation, not a one-shot request. Effective Claude users treat first drafts as starting points, give specific feedback ("cut the first two paragraphs and make the conclusion more action-oriented" beats "make it shorter"), and know when to start fresh rather than fight a conversation that's gone off track.

What is AI Fluency?

AI Fluency is the ability to collaborate effectively with AI — not just knowing which buttons to click, but developing judgment for different situations. The 4D Framework (Dakan & Feller) names four competencies:

  • Delegation — deciding what work goes to humans vs. AI, and how to distribute tasks.
  • Description — communicating effectively: defining outputs, guiding process, specifying behavior.
  • Discernment — critically evaluating AI outputs for quality, accuracy, and appropriateness.
  • Diligence — using AI responsibly and ethically, with transparency and accountability.

The prompt framework from the previous lesson is rooted in Description; the troubleshooting table above draws on Discernment and Diligence.

Evaluating Claude for your workflows

Evals are a systematic way to develop intuition for how Claude performs on tasks that matter to you: (1) gather 5–10 examples of a task you already do, (2) write prompts that would generate similar outputs, (3) compare Claude's outputs to your examples — does it capture the key information, is the tone right, what's missing, (4) refine your prompts based on the gap.

Projects

Introduction to projects

Projects are self-contained workspaces with their own memory, chat histories, knowledge bases, and customized instructions — dedicated environments for specific work streams.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain what projects are and when to use them
  • Create a new project with a name, description, and visibility settings
  • Add documents and files to your project's knowledge base
  • Write effective project instructions to guide Claude's behavior
  • Share projects with teammates (for Claude for Work (Team and Enterprise plan) users)

Key takeaways

  • Project knowledge lets you upload documents Claude references across every chat in the project — no re-uploading each time.
  • Project instructions guide tone, expertise level, and response style for every conversation inside.
  • Projects scale automatically. As the knowledge base approaches context limits, Claude switches to searching your project knowledge (RAG) and pulling in only what's relevant — expanding capacity by up to 10x while maintaining response quality.
  • For Claude for Work users, projects enable collaboration — share the same context, instructions, and knowledge with teammates.

When to use projects

Projects are particularly valuable for ongoing work, not just a one-off question. Consider creating one when you have:

  • Reference materials you'll use repeatedly (meeting notes, survey results, reports, historical data).
  • Consistent requirements for how Claude should respond (always formal language, always cite sources, always follow a template).
  • Team collaboration needs where multiple people should work from the same foundation.

Setting one up

  1. Set up your project. New Project → name it descriptively, add a short description (helps you and teammates, not seen directly by Claude), choose visibility (private or org-shared).
  2. Add project instructions. Tell Claude how to behave across every conversation: context ("this project is for marketing content for our B2B product"), process ("first draft a structure, then write"), tone/style, and specific requirements ("always include a call-to-action"). You can also automate workflows, e.g. "when I upload a meeting transcript, summarize it with this template."
  3. Build the knowledge base. Upload reference documents, background materials, examples of work to emulate, and technical specs (PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, HTML, or connect Google Drive). Name files descriptively — "Q4-2024-Brand-Guidelines.pdf" beats "document1.pdf," since Claude uses file names to retrieve the right information.

Projects scale via Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG): once the knowledge base approaches the context limit, Claude stops loading everything at once and instead searches your files for what's relevant — a visual indicator shows when a project is RAG-enabled, but the experience feels the same.

Once set up, every conversation inside the project automatically has access to its knowledge base and follows its instructions.

To share: open the project, click "Share project," then add members by name/email (individually or as a bulk-pasted list) or share with "Everyone at [your organization]" to make it discoverable in the Team tab. Invited teammates get an email notification and find it under "Shared with me."

Example projects

  • Q4 product launch — specs, competitive analysis, messaging notes.
  • Research support — competitive review, user research, customer feedback.
  • Client account hub — brand guidelines, past deliverables, communication history.
  • Event planning workspace — venue contracts, speaker bios, attendee data.
  • Job description generator — past job descriptions, team charters, headcount docs.

Best practices. Start focused, then expand. Keep knowledge current. Write clear instructions. Name documents descriptively and group related files. Reference documents by name in your prompts.

Creating your first artifact

Creating an artifact is as simple as having a conversation. Just describe what you want, and Claude will determine whether to present it as an artifact. For example, you might say:

  • "Create a flowchart showing our customer onboarding process."
  • "Build an interactive dashboard that lets me input monthly expenses and see a breakdown."
  • "Design a landing page for a productivity app with a hero section and feature list."
  • "Write a project brief template I can reuse for new initiatives."

  • View different formats — toggle between a preview (how it looks) and the underlying code.
  • Copy content — click the copy icon to grab the content for use elsewhere.
  • Download files — save the artifact as a file to your computer.
  • View code — see exactly what Claude generated under the hood.

Sharing and publishing

  • Copy or download — for personal use or sharing via other channels.
  • Share within your organization (Claude for Work) — stays internal, requires team authentication.
  • Publish publicly (Free, Pro, Max) — anyone with the link can view and interact without an account, and can "remix" it into their own conversation. Only the selected version becomes public; your chat stays private. Published artifacts aren't indexed by search engines.

Tips. Be specific about what you want. Describe the end user. Iterate incrementally, one change at a time. If Claude answers in chat instead of creating an artifact, just say "Please create that as an artifact."

Skills

Working with skills

Skills are folders of instructions, scripts, and resources that Claude loads dynamically to improve performance on specialized tasks — expertise packages that teach Claude how to complete a task in a repeatable way.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain what Skills are and how Claude uses them
  • Identify Anthropic's built-in Skills for document creation
  • Enable and manage Skills in your settings

Skills are currently a feature preview for Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans. If you're on the Free plan, you can read along to understand the concept and skip the hands-on steps.

Say you regularly create the same type of Excel report, PowerPoint deck, or Word document — each with the same structure, formatting, and conventions. Instead of re-explaining those conventions every time, a Skill packages them once so Claude applies them automatically whenever it's relevant. Skills can also encode a custom workflow entirely of your own design — the specific steps, order of operations, and methodology you want followed every time.

Types of Skills

Anthropic Skills
Created and maintained by Anthropic, these give Claude enhanced document creation for Excel, Word, PowerPoint, and PDF files. They're available to all paid users, and Claude invokes them automatically when relevant — no setup required beyond enabling them.
Custom Skills
Ones you or your organization create for your own workflows — brand guidelines applied to presentations, a meeting-notes structure, a data-analysis workflow, a quarterly variance methodology. You teach Claude your process once, and it follows that process consistently every time you invoke the skill.

Enabling Skills

To use Skills, you first need to turn on the underlying capability, then enable the specific Skills you want available:

  1. Go to Settings > Capabilities.
  2. Toggle on Code execution and file creation.
  3. Scroll down to the Skills section.
  4. Toggle on the individual Skills you want to use.

On Team and Enterprise plans, workspace admins may control which Skills are available to the organization. Once enabled, you'll see the available Skills listed in your settings, and Claude will draw on them automatically in relevant conversations.

Using Skills in practice

Once enabled, you can simply ask for the output you need and Claude will apply the relevant Skill:

  • "Create an Excel spreadsheet tracking our Q3 expenses by department."
  • "Turn these meeting notes into a PowerPoint summary for the leadership team."
  • "Generate a PDF report from this data with our standard formatting."
  • "Build a financial model in Excel with scenario analysis."

You'll be able to see Claude's chain of thought as it works through the task, and the finished file is available to download when it's done.

Claude works with you on slides, spreadsheets, and contract redlines

Skills let Claude work with your actual files in a contained environment — not just describe what a file should contain. Upload a .xlsx, .pptx, .docx, or .pdf, and Claude can edit it directly, then hand back a file you can download or open in Drive. If the task calls for pulling in external data sources, you'll need to toggle on "Allow limited network access" for Claude to reach them.

Security considerations

  • Only install custom Skills from trusted sources.
  • Anthropic's built-in Skills are tested and maintained by Anthropic.
  • Custom Skills you upload are private to your individual account.
  • If installing a Skill from an external source, review its contents first.

Creating custom skills

You don't need to write a Skill file by hand — Claude builds it with you through conversation:

  1. Tell Claude what you want: "I want a skill for writing quarterly business reviews."
  2. Answer Claude's questions about your workflow and what good output looks like.
  3. Upload reference materials — templates, style guides, examples you're proud of.
  4. Save the skill; Claude generates the structured file and it's ready to use.

Find your Skills under the Customize tab. Your custom Skill appears alongside Anthropic's built-in Skills, and you can ask Claude to edit it any time to improve it with iteration.

Skills vs. Projects

You might be wondering — if both skills and projects can be used to give more context to Claude, when should I use each? Think of it this way: projects store knowledge, skills perform tasks.

Projects are knowledge hubs. They hold the reference materials Claude needs to understand your work — project specs, meeting notes, research documents. When you upload files to a project, Claude draws on that information across every conversation within that project.

Skills are procedural machines. They encode how Claude should execute a task — the specific steps, order of operations, and methodology you want followed every time. Skills shine when you have repeatable workflows you want Claude to run consistently.

The two features complement each other. A skill can reference knowledge stored in a project — your "customer call prep" skill might pull from customer profiles uploaded to a project's knowledge base. The project provides the what (information), the skill provides the how (process).

Setting up Enterprise Search

Enterprise Search requires a two-step setup process: first an admin configures it for the organization, then individual users authenticate with their personal accounts.

Key takeaways

  • Research transforms how Claude finds and analyzes information. What is Research?

Research is an advanced feature that transforms Claude from a conversational assistant into a systematic investigator. When you enable Research, Claude doesn't just answer your question — it explores it from multiple angles, synthesizing information from across the web and your connected integrations.

Think of it as having a skilled research assistant who gathers information, cross-references sources, and compiles a comprehensive report while you stay on your own work.

Research is particularly valuable when you need more than a quick answer. It's designed for situations where a thorough understanding requires pulling together information from multiple sources, comparing different perspectives, and synthesizing findings into actionable insights.

When to use Research

Understanding when to use Research versus other Claude capabilities helps you get the best results for your specific needs.

Try it · Research or not?

Route each question to the right tool: Research, Quick web search, Thinking, or Enterprise search.

Q1

"Compare the three payroll providers we're considering — pricing, implementation time, and support quality — and give me sources I can check."

Research. A comparative analysis needing information from multiple sources, synthesized into one answer, with verifiable citations — all four signals point to Research. A single search can't compare three vendors on three criteria.

Q2

"I'm prepping the quarterly investor update — what's our biggest competitor's current stock price?"

Quick web search. The question itself is a single fact with one authoritative source. When speed matters more than comprehensiveness, web search is the right size.

Q3

"This SQL query keeps returning duplicate rows and I can't see why. Walk through the logic and find the bug."

Thinking. Everything needed is already in the question — the answer comes from reasoning through the query's logic, not gathering outside information. Code debugging, math, and logical analysis are Thinking's home ground.

Q4

"Plan our 20-person team offsite: compare two or three city options with venues, rough costs, and travel logistics."

Research. Planning a complex project means pulling together many sources — venues, prices, logistics — and synthesizing them into something actionable: thorough-investigation territory that would otherwise take hours by hand.

Q5

"What's our travel reimbursement policy for international trips?"

Enterprise search. The answer lives in your organization's own documents, not the public web — company knowledge is exactly what Enterprise Search draws from. Research investigates the outside world; your policies live inside.

How Research works

  1. Plans its approach — breaks down the request and identifies what it needs to investigate.
  2. Conducts multiple searches — determines what to investigate next based on what it finds.
  3. Synthesizes findings — compiles everything into one comprehensive, organized report.
  4. Provides citations — every claim links back to its source.

Using Research in practice

  3. Enter your prompt and submit
  4. Claude will work in the background, and you'll see progress indicators as it searches and analyzes

Tips for effective Research prompts

Since a Research run takes minutes rather than seconds, investing time in crafting your prompt pays off. Here are some strategies:

  • Be specific about your goals. Instead of "Tell me about the EV market," try "Analyze the electric vehicle battery market — identify key players, technology trends, and supply chain challenges that might affect investment decisions."
  • Specify the sections or structure you want. Claude will organize its findings around the structure you provide. For example: "Compare venue options for a team offsite including: location and accessibility, meeting space and amenities, catering options, and pricing considerations."
  • Include relevant constraints. Budget ranges, timelines, geographic requirements, and other parameters help Claude focus its research on relevant options.
  • Ask Claude to help refine your prompt. If you're not sure how to frame your research question, you can even ask Claude to help you write a better Research prompt before enabling the feature.

Working with connected integrations

When you have Google Workspace or other integrations connected, Research becomes even more powerful. Claude can pull context from your emails, calendar, and documents alongside web research.

For example, you might ask Claude to:

  • "Summarize what's been discussed about Project X across my emails and Slack, then research industry best practices for similar initiatives"
  • "Review my calendar commitments for next week and research each company I'm meeting with"
  • "Find all internal documents about our pricing strategy and compare to how competitors are positioning themselves"

When using Research with integrations, you can steer Claude by saying things like "Pull relevant context from my Google Drive" or "Include insights from my recent emails on this topic."

  • You're exploring design directions and want to generate and compare several variations quickly

What happens when your project knowledge base approaches context limits?

  1. Claude stops accepting new file uploads
  2. Older files are automatically deleted to make room
  3. You must create a new project to continue
  4. Claude searches your project knowledge and retrieves only what's relevant, expanding capacity by up to 10x

Why D. This is Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) — when the knowledge base nears the context limit, Claude retrieves only the relevant parts of your documents rather than loading everything, expanding effective capacity up to 10x.

Čo sa stane, keď sa znalostná báza vášho projektu priblíži k limitu kontextu?

  1. Claude prestane prijímať nové nahrávanie súborov
  2. Staršie súbory sa automaticky vymažú, aby sa uvoľnilo miesto
  3. Na pokračovanie musíte vytvoriť nový projekt
  4. Claude prehľadá znalosti projektu a načíta iba to relevantné, čím rozšíri kapacitu až 10-násobne

Prečo D. Ide o Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) — keď sa znalostná báza priblíži k limitu kontextu, Claude načíta iba relevantné časti dokumentov namiesto ich celého nahratia, čím rozšíri efektívnu kapacitu až 10-násobne.

Q5

What can you do with a published artifact?

  1. Share it with anyone via link, and others can "remix" it in their own Claude conversations
  2. Only view it — no interaction is possible
  3. It automatically gets posted to social media
  4. It becomes permanently locked and cannot be modified

Why A. Publishing makes the selected version accessible via link to anyone, without a Claude account, and lets others remix it into their own conversation to modify and build on it.

Čo môžete urobiť s publikovaným artifactom?

  1. Zdieľať ho s kýmkoľvek cez odkaz, pričom ostatní si ho môžu „remixnúť" do vlastných konverzácií s Claude
  2. Iba si ho pozrieť — interakcia nie je možná
  3. Automaticky sa zverejní na sociálnych sieťach
  4. Trvalo sa zamkne a nedá sa upraviť

Prečo A. Publikovanie sprístupní zvolenú verziu cez odkaz komukoľvek bez účtu Claude a umožní ostatným si ju remixnúť do vlastnej konverzácie, aby ju upravili a rozvíjali ďalej.

Q6

What is the primary difference between projects and skills?

  1. Projects are free, skills require a paid plan
  2. Projects store knowledge, skills perform tasks
  3. Projects are for teams, skills are for individuals
  4. Projects work offline, skills require internet

Why B. Projects are knowledge hubs — reference materials Claude draws on. Skills are procedural machines — they encode how Claude should execute a task, step by step, the same way every time.

Aký je hlavný rozdiel medzi projects a skills?

  1. Projects sú zadarmo, skills vyžadujú platený plán
  2. Projects ukladajú znalosti, skills vykonávajú úlohy
  3. Projects sú pre tímy, skills pre jednotlivcov
  4. Projects fungujú offline, skills vyžadujú internet

Prečo B. Projects sú znalostné huby — referenčné materiály, z ktorých Claude čerpá. Skills sú procedurálne stroje — kódujú, ako má Claude úlohu vykonať, krok za krokom, rovnako pri každom spustení.

Q7

What data can Claude access through connectors?

  1. Only data created in the last 30 days

  3. Only data you have permission to access
  4. Only docs that have been individually shared with Anthropic

Why C. Claude sees what you see. Connecting your work email doesn't give Claude access to your CEO's inbox — only your own permissions carry over.

Ku ktorým dátam môže Claude pristupovať cez konektory?

  3. Iba k dátam, ku ktorým máte vy oprávnenie
  4. Iba k dokumentom, ktoré boli individuálne zdieľané s Anthropic

Prečo C. Claude vidí to, čo vidíte vy. Pripojenie pracovného e-mailu nedáva Claude prístup do schránky vášho CEO — prenášajú sa iba vaše vlastné oprávnenia.

Q9

Ktorá z nasledujúcich úloh je dobrým kandidátom na Research?
